The γ in your statistical factor comes from one assumption, and with standard relativity it cancels out

Your derivation adds γ to the exponent by taking E = γε out of the reservoir while keeping the reservoir's energy scale at mc² in the external frame. If you take out ε instead, or treat the multiplicity as frame-invariant (which standard relativity requires), γ drops out and the internal statistics do not change with common motion.

A · Your derivation
ΩR/Ω0 = e−γε/mc²
Removes γε in the external frame, with ∂lnΩ/∂K = 1/mc² also taken in the external frame.
B · Remove ε, not γε
ΩR/Ω0 = e−ε/mc²
Same fixed scale, with the internal energy removed. No dependence on v. Equivalent to doing the whole derivation in the rest frame.
C · Standard relativity
ΩR/Ω0 = e−γε/(γmc²) = e−ε/mc²
Removes γε in the external frame, but lnΩ counts microstates, so it is invariant. The external-frame scale therefore becomes γmc², and the γ cancels.

In case A, a moving system would be able to measure its own speed from the populations of its internal states. Case C rules this out, which is the principle of relativity.

4 · Where γε goes, as seen from the external frame

When a system moving at a fixed v gains internal energy ε, its rest mass rises by ε/c². The external observer sees an energy increase of γε and a momentum increase of γvε/c². Only ε of that energy reorganises the inside. The rest is the kinetic energy needed to keep the added mass moving at v.

(i) Energy from a reservoir moving with the system. v is held fixed, so γ does not change. Dilation is conserved and the internal populations are unchanged (case C). From outside, the internal transition rates are slowed by 1/γ, just as before the transfer.

(ii) Energy taken from the horizontal motion. This needs something outside the system to take up the momentum. The quantity conserved is γMc², so if the inside gains ε, γ has to fall: γ₂ = γ₁/(1+ε/Mc²). Dilation is reduced, and by exactly the amount that rest energy increased.

(iii) Isolated system. E and p are both conserved, so the invariant mass √(E²−p²c²)/c² is conserved. An isolated system cannot turn its own horizontal kinetic energy into internal reorganisation. ε = 0 and dilation is conserved trivially.

Scope

All of your algebra, from step 1 through step 13, is correct once its premises are granted. The 1/γ comes from condition 3, which applies ∂lnΩ/∂K = 1/mc² to the reservoir in the external frame. Standard relativity treats entropy as invariant, so in that frame the slope becomes 1/(γmc²). Case A should therefore be read as a proposed departure from relativity. It makes a definite prediction, motion-dependent internal populations, which would show up in experiments on fast ions and particles. On scale: for an N₂ molecule, mc² is about 10¹² times kBT at room temperature, so at ordinary energies all three curves sit at ≈ 1.
